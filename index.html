Sedation Dentistry - What is Sedation Dentistry?

January 14th, 2013

Sedation dentistry refers to the use of medications to help patients feel calm and relaxed before and during a dental appointment. Different methods can be used depending on the procedure, patient needs and a dental professional's assessment.

Options that patients may hear about include nitrous oxide (sometimes called laughing gas), oral sedation and intravenous sedation. Ask a qualified dental professional about benefits, potential risks and appropriate supervision.

Pain Free Dentistry - The Methods Applied In Pain Free Dentistry In Colorado

January 14th, 2013

In the past, a visit to the dentist was dreaded by many people because of the discomfort associated with treatment. Methods designed to improve patient comfort have developed over time, including local anesthesia and technology used during certain dental procedures.

Lasers may be used for selected procedures involving soft tissues, such as the gums. The method used and the level of discomfort vary by treatment and patient; no procedure can be guaranteed to be entirely pain-free.

Crowns On Teeth - Connecting Crowns On Teeth With CEREC

January 14th, 2013

The options available for damaged or unhealthy teeth vary depending on clinical needs and what a local dental practitioner offers. Technology has developed to support digital impressions and some same-day crown fabrication methods, including CEREC.

Dental crowns may be recommended to restore the structure or function of teeth that are cracked, weakened or affected by decay. A dentist can explain preparation, materials, suitability and alternatives.

Family Dentist - Find A Good Family Dentist In The Area

September 14th, 2012

Dental hygiene, like any other type of hygiene, is important. People moving into a new area often look for a family dentist who can offer routine examinations and ongoing care. Availability of services depends on the local area and individual practice.

When comparing practices, consider services provided for different age groups, accessibility, insurance arrangements, and whether the dentist answers questions clearly.

Infection Control - This Has Always Been Problematic, Infection Control Especially In Health Care Facilities

July 14th, 2012

Patients and healthcare professionals expect high levels of cleanliness in healthcare facilities. Infection prevention involves more than visibly clean surfaces; it also addresses microorganisms and how they can spread during care.

Dental offices use measures such as instrument sterilization, hand hygiene, protective equipment and cleaning procedures to help reduce the risk of infection.
